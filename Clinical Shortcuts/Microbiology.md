# **LABORATORY DIAGNOSIS OF VIRAL INFECTIONS**

For every viral infection, organize laboratory diagnosis systematically under:

Specimen → Direct detection → Antigen detection → Serology → Molecular methods → Virus isolation

Use only the relevant methods for the specific virus.

1. DIRECT DEMONSTRATION

Electron microscopy

- Demonstrates characteristic viral morphology.
- Immunoelectron microscopy can improve specificity using specific antibodies.

Immunofluorescence

- Direct fluorescent antibody test detects viral antigen directly in clinical specimens.

Light microscopy / Histopathology

- Demonstrates characteristic viral inclusion bodies.
- Examples:
  - Rabies → Negri bodies
  - CMV → Owl's-eye inclusions
  - Measles → Warthin-Finkeldey giant cells

2. ANTIGEN DETECTION

Methods:

- ELISA
- Immunochromatographic tests (ICT)
- ELFA
- Direct fluorescent antibody tests

Examples:

- Dengue → NS1 antigen
- HIV → p24 antigen
- Rotavirus → Stool antigen

3. SEROLOGY

IgM
→ Usually indicates recent/acute infection.

IgG
→ Indicates previous exposure, immunity or chronic infection depending on the virus.

Four-fold or greater rise in IgG titre between acute and convalescent sera
→ Evidence of recent infection.

Common methods:

- ELISA
- IFA
- Neutralization tests
- Hemagglutination inhibition
- Complement fixation
- Immunoblotting where appropriate

4. MOLECULAR METHODS

Nucleic-acid amplification tests are highly sensitive and specific and are widely used for viral diagnosis.

PCR
→ Detects viral DNA.

RT-PCR
→ Detects RNA viruses after reverse transcription.

Real-time PCR/RT-PCR
→ Detects and quantifies viral nucleic acid.
→ Useful for determining viral load and monitoring treatment response.

Multiplex PCR
→ Simultaneously detects multiple pathogens from a single clinical specimen.

5. VIRUS ISOLATION

Viruses are obligate intracellular parasites and therefore cannot be cultivated on ordinary cell-free artificial media.

Cell culture

- Primary cell cultures
- Diploid/secondary cell cultures
- Continuous cell lines

Viral growth may be detected by:

- Cytopathic effect (CPE)
- Hemadsorption
- Immunological detection of viral antigen
- Molecular detection

Examples of CPE:

- Cell rounding
- Ballooning
- Syncytium formation
- Cell destruction

Embryonated eggs and laboratory animals

- Historically important.
- Now mainly used for selected viruses, research and vaccine production.

IMPORTANT VIRUS-SPECIFIC DIAGNOSTIC CLUES

RABIES VIRUS

- Postmortem brain tissue → Negri bodies
- Antigen detection → Direct fluorescent antibody test
- Antemortem diagnosis may use samples such as nuchal skin biopsy, saliva or CSF depending on the test.
- Electron microscopy → Bullet-shaped virus

CYTOMEGALOVIRUS (CMV)

- Histopathology → Owl's-eye intranuclear inclusions
- Rapid culture → Shell-vial technique
- Antigen detection and PCR are commonly used.

SARS-CoV-2

- Main diagnostic method → Real-time RT-PCR/NAAT
- Antigen detection → Rapid antigen tests
- Serology → Useful mainly for evidence of previous immune response rather than routine diagnosis of acute infection.
- Electron microscopy → Coronavirus has characteristic crown-like surface spikes.

HIV

- Screening/initial diagnosis → HIV antigen-antibody immunoassay
- Early infection → p24 antigen and/or HIV nucleic-acid testing
- Current confirmation uses HIV-1/HIV-2 differentiation immunoassay and, when indicated, nucleic-acid testing.
- Do not routinely write Western blot as the current confirmatory test.

DENGUE VIRUS

Early infection:
→ NS1 antigen detection

Later/recent infection:
→ Dengue-specific IgM, commonly by MAC-ELISA

Molecular:
→ RT-PCR during the early viremic phase

ROTAVIRUS

- Stool antigen detection → ELISA/rapid immunoassay
- RT-PCR → Molecular confirmation
- Electron microscopy → Wheel-shaped virus
- Routine culture is not generally used.

MEASLES VIRUS

- Serology → Measles-specific IgM
- Molecular → RT-PCR
- Cell culture may demonstrate:
  → Multinucleated giant cells
  → Intranuclear and intracytoplasmic inclusions
- Warthin-Finkeldey giant cells are a classic histological clue.

EPSTEIN-BARR VIRUS (EBV)

- Heterophile antibody test → Paul-Bunnell/Monospot-type testing
- EBV-specific serology:
  - VCA-IgM → acute/recent infection
  - VCA-IgG → current or previous infection
  - EBNA antibodies → past infection
- Molecular testing → EBV DNA PCR when clinically indicated.
